Myth 8 · 2 min read

"Bitcoin gets hacked."

By Banitama · Reviewed by Ricky Saif · Updated October 21, 2026

Example draft. This page shows the planned format. The author will replace it with their own words and experience before it is final.
The short answer

The Bitcoin network itself has not been broken. The losses people read about came from exchanges, wallets and people, which is why custody design matters more than the technology.

What people are really asking

Headlines about stolen coins and collapsed exchanges make Bitcoin sound unsafe. Leaders want to know whether their money could be taken.

What is true in the myth

Money has been stolen. Exchanges have been hacked and some have failed, taking customer funds with them. Mt. Gox in 2014 and FTX in 2022 are the best known. Individuals have lost coins to phishing, malware and fake apps. These are real losses and any serious plan assumes they can happen.

Where the myth breaks down

Separate the layers. The network, meaning the protocol and its ledger, has run continuously for years without anyone rewriting its history or creating coins outside its rules. Almost every loss happened one layer above, in a company that held other people’s coins, or in a person who lost control of their keys. Think of the difference between a bank vault and a bank’s website.

As a software developer, I look at it this way: the weak point is nearly always the human and the process. A single person holding a single key on a single laptop is a single point of failure. So is trusting a platform whose books you cannot see.

How the risk is reduced

  • Do not leave funds on a trading platform. Hold them in custody built for the purpose.
  • Use more than one key. A multi-signature setup requires several approvals to move funds, so one stolen or lost key is not enough.
  • Split roles. The person who proposes a payment should not be the one who approves it.
  • Test recovery. A backup you have never restored is a hope, not a plan.
  • Keep records. Know who can do what, and write down what happens when someone leaves.
