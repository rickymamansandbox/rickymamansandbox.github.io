Myth 5 · 2 min read

"Gold is the safer hedge."

By Banitama · Reviewed by Ricky Saif · Updated October 21, 2026

Example draft. This page shows the planned format. The author will replace it with their own words and experience before it is final.
The short answer

Gold has the longest track record as a store of value, and for that reason it is hard to beat. Bitcoin is not a replacement; it differs on supply, portability and verification, and the sensible comparison is what each protects you from.

What people are really asking

Gold has held value for thousands of years. Why trust something that has existed since 2009?

What is true in the myth

Gold’s record is unmatched. It has survived empires, currencies and wars, and central banks still hold it. Its long history makes it a trusted hedge, and dismissing it would be a mistake. If your concern is the lowest possible model risk, gold has the strongest claim.

Where the myth breaks down

The better comparison is on the properties that matter for a store of value:

Property Gold Bitcoin
Supply New gold is mined every year, adding roughly 1 to 2 percent to the stock Capped at 21 million, issuance is public and falling
Portability Heavy and costly to move or secure at scale Can move across borders in minutes
Verification Needs assay and trusted vaults to confirm purity and existence Anyone can verify balances and rules themselves
Divisibility Hard to divide small amounts Divisible to eight decimal places
Track record Thousands of years Under two decades
Custody risk Physical theft, counterparty vaults Key management, software and custodians

Reading monetary history, the pattern is that money moves to whatever is hardest to debase and easiest to move. Gold won that role when the alternative was shells and salt. The question for Bitcoin is whether it does the same job better in a digital economy, and it has a much shorter record to prove it.

A better question

Not “gold or Bitcoin?”, but “what is each for?” Gold is a slow, proven hedge. Bitcoin is a newer, more volatile bet on a monetary network. They fail in different ways, and some institutions hold both.

What we do with your team

We set the two side by side against your own concerns, whether that is inflation, sanctions risk, cross-border payments or simply diversification, and let the facts choose the weight.
