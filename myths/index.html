Myth busting

How we respond to myths about Bitcoin.

Anyone can say they understand Bitcoin. The quickest way to assess a partner is to read how they respond to the myths everyone has heard. Below is how we answer the common ones: plainly, without hype, and with the trade-offs left in. Use it to judge the quality of our thinking before you ever book a call.

Myth 1 · Ricky Saif

"It's too late; Bitcoin is a bubble."

Price and adoption are different questions. A bubble needs a reason to disappear; Bitcoin has a fixed supply and a growing base of holders, which is why the better question is how much risk you can live with, not whether you missed it.

Myth 2 · Ricky Saif

"Inflation is under control; our cash is fine."

Cash loses purchasing power every year even when inflation is low. Low inflation is slower erosion, not none, and idle reserves are where it quietly adds up.

Read our answer →
Myth 3 · Ricky Saif

"Our board will never approve this."

Boards do not reject Bitcoin; they reject unclear risk. A short decision paper with limits, custody, accounting and an exit rule answers most of what a board asks.

Read our answer →
Myth 4 · Faizal Abdillah

"It's too volatile for a company treasury."

Volatility is real, and it is a reason to size the position, not to ignore the asset. The sensible response is a capped allocation, a long time horizon and written limits.

Read our answer →
Myth 5 · Banitama

"Gold is the safer hedge."

Gold has the longest track record as a store of value, and for that reason it is hard to beat. Bitcoin is not a replacement; it differs on supply, portability and verification, and the sensible comparison is what each protects you from.

Read our answer →
Myth 6 · Faizal Abdillah

"We can't audit or account for it."

Companies already audit and report Bitcoin holdings. The work is in valuation, proof of ownership and disclosure, and accounting standards now cover it directly in several jurisdictions.

Read our answer →
Myth 7 · Faizal Abdillah

"Regulators won't allow companies to hold it."

In most markets a company may hold Bitcoin; the rules usually target the platforms that trade and store it. Because rules differ by country and change often, the right step is a checklist for your own lawyer, not a yes or no.

Read our answer →
Myth 8 · Banitama

"Bitcoin gets hacked."

The Bitcoin network itself has not been broken. The losses people read about came from exchanges, wallets and people, which is why custody design matters more than the technology.

Read our answer →
Myth 9 · Banitama

"If we lose the keys, it's gone."

This is true if custody is poorly designed. With shared approvals, tested backups and written procedures, no single lost key, laptop or person can lock a company out.

Read our answer →
Myth 10 · Banitama

"Bitcoin wastes energy."

Bitcoin mining uses a lot of electricity by design, because that cost is what secures the network. Whether it is waste depends on the energy source and on what you compare it with, and both are worth looking at with real numbers.
