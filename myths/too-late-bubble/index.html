Myth 1 · 2 min read

"It's too late; Bitcoin is a bubble."

By Ricky Saif · Reviewed by Faizal Abdillah · Updated October 21, 2026

Example draft. This page shows the planned format. The author will replace it with their own words and experience before it is final.
The short answer

Price and adoption are different questions. A bubble needs a reason to disappear; Bitcoin has a fixed supply and a growing base of holders, which is why the better question is how much risk you can live with, not whether you missed it.

What people are really asking

When a leader says “it’s too late”, they usually mean two things. The price has already gone up, and they don’t want to be the last one in. Both worries are fair. Neither one answers the question a treasury needs answered.

What is true in the myth

Bitcoin’s price has risen and fallen sharply more than once, and some buyers have lost money by buying at the wrong time. Anyone who tells you it only goes up is selling something. A responsible plan assumes the price can fall by a large amount and still works.

Where the myth breaks down

A bubble is a price detached from anything that supports it. So ask what supports Bitcoin. Its supply is capped at 21 million coins and the schedule is public. More institutions, funds and companies now hold it than when it launched in 2009. US spot Bitcoin exchange-traded funds began trading in January 2024, which put it inside ordinary brokerage accounts. None of this guarantees a price. It does mean the asset is not a passing fad that vanishes overnight.

“Too late” also assumes the only way to win is to buy at the bottom. A treasury is not a trade. If you are protecting purchasing power over ten years, the question is whether the asset can hold value against the currency you keep your cash in, and whether you can hold it through bad years.

A better question

Instead of “have we missed it?”, ask three things:

  1. What share of our cash reserves could we hold in an asset that may fall by half and still sleep well?
  2. Over what time horizon are we judging it?
  3. Who will own the decision, and what would make us sell?

Those questions turn a fear into a policy. That is exactly what a leadership education session is for.

What we do with your team

We start by writing down each leader’s worry in their own words, then answer them one at a time, with sources. Most teams find the conversation shifts from “is it too late?” to “how much, and under what rules?”
